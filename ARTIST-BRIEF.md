# Satoshi Stacks — artist brief

Create a desktop Bitcoin poker room with a Blade Runner–inspired cyberpunk,
post-apocalyptic bunker atmosphere: worn steel, dark concrete, exposed wiring,
subtle haze, amber emergency lights, cyan neon, and small magenta accents.
It should feel like an underground poker club that survived the collapse.
Use original designs, without film logos or copied scenery.

- Make the large six-seat oval table the focal point. Restore our existing
  “Satoshi Stacks / Open All Nite” neon sign as a much larger centerpiece on
  the felt. Keep its orange, cyan, and pink identity. Deliver its glow separately.
- Keep the center behind community cards and the pot visually quiet, with
  room around all six seats for avatars, names, cards, and bets. Supply an
  uncluttered felt version so the sign can be dimmed during hands.
- Design one matching card back and a full 52-card deck in the same world.
  Keep familiar suits, clear corner ranks, and strong contrast at small sizes;
  put the richest artwork in the face cards and backs. No jokers needed.
- Build for desktop windows from small to ultrawide/4K. The table and seats
  scale together without stretching; the room background may crop. Do not
  bake game controls, player labels, cards, or chips into the room/table art.
- Assets must be interchangeable live: deliver room background, felt, rail,
  logo, and glow separately. Every alternate table theme must use the same
  shape, proportions, transparent bounds, and clear areas for gameplay. Use
  the coordinate templates in `TABLE-ASSETS.md`; backgrounds can crop, but
  table layers must align exactly. Keep card dimensions and corner indices
  identical across all deck designs.
- Deliver layered editable source files and separate transparent exports for
  room details, table/felt, rail, sign, and glow. Provide SVG for scalable art
  where practical, plus high-resolution PNG/WebP assets (room at least 3840 ×
  2160; table at least 3000 px wide). All cards must share one aspect ratio
  and consistent corner placement. Include commercial usage rights.

First milestone: one room/table concept, one card back, and one face card,
shown in our actual game layout at 1440 × 900 and a small desktop window.
Approve the direction before completing the room and full deck.
